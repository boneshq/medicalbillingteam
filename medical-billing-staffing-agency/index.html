Medical billing staffing agency: choose the people and the operating model

By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published November 7, 2026 · Updated November 7, 2026 · How we research and review

This site is operated by Alliance Global Solutions, a provider of remote medical billing and revenue cycle staff. We may contact you about our services if you request it.

Short answer: A medical billing staffing agency helps you recruit people for defined billing work. Dedicated remote staff fit practices and billing companies that already have someone to manage the revenue cycle and need more capacity in their own systems. Before comparing agencies, define the queue, the supervisor, required payer experience, working hours and the full cost of the arrangement.

Need billing staff? Request a staffing assessment with your specialty, systems, approximate workload and coverage hours. Include business information only; do not send patient details, claim files or screenshots containing patient information.

Staffing agency or billing company: which do you need?

The label on a provider's website tells you less than the contract. Some providers offer more than one model. Ask who owns each task and who is accountable when work stalls.

Hire for the queue that needs attention

"Medical biller" can cover several different jobs. A precise brief makes interviews more useful and reduces the risk of hiring someone whose experience is in a different part of the cycle.

Work to assignExperience to demonstrateExample work sample using fictional data
A/R follow-upPayer portals, follow-up notes and next-action trackingPrioritize five unpaid claims and explain the next step for each

Keep coding decisions, appeals, write-offs and patient-balance changes within the authority you explicitly assign. The remote biller hiring guide includes interview questions and a sample onboarding sequence.

Give every agency the same six-part brief

  1. Work: the queues included and the tasks excluded.
  2. Volume: an approximate count of incoming work and existing backlog, with the period stated.
  3. Context: specialty, major payer types, practice management system and clearinghouse.
  4. Coverage: required time zone, hours and payer-call availability.
  5. Management: who sets priorities, answers questions and reviews work.
  6. Evidence: candidate assessment, sample reporting, security documentation and references you want to review.

Use the medical billing RFP template and vendor scorecard to collect comparable written responses. A proposed start date should account for recruiting, access setup and client-led training.

Compare total cost before the headline rate

Build the comparison around the same role, hours and responsibilities. Ask the provider to identify which costs are included and which remain yours.

Monthly comparison: quoted staffing fee + software and clearinghouse seats + secure access costs + your supervision time + any separately billed onboarding or coverage costs.

Then check minimum term, overtime, holidays, replacement terms, notice period and exit fees. Compare that total with an in-house employee using the staffing cost guide and state wage and benefit calculator. Public wage estimates are a baseline; they are not an AGS quote or a guarantee of savings.

Verify the access arrangement before onboarding

Outsourced billing involving protected health information generally creates a business-associate relationship. HHS explains the written agreement requirements, including safeguards, subcontractors and handling information at contract end. Review the applicable arrangement with your compliance lead before granting access. HHS business associate contracts.

Ask the agency to walk through an actual onboarding and offboarding process: named accounts, permission approval, device controls, incident contacts and access removal. Confirm staff locations and any payer or customer-contract restrictions that affect the proposed team. Our HIPAA and outsourced billing guide offers additional questions.

Agree how the first month will be evaluated

Choose a manageable queue and record the starting backlog. Agree on how quality will be sampled, what a completed item means, and where blocked work is reported. Review those measures alongside throughput; closing a task in a queue does not necessarily mean a claim was paid.

Ask for a written ownership map covering the agency contact, your supervisor and the person authorized to approve exceptions. Use the first review to identify training gaps and process problems before expanding the assignment. Avoid accepting a promised collections increase without a defined baseline, time period and explanation of what the provider controls.

Talk through your staffing requirement

Request an assessment from the AGS team or review the AGS vendor profile. Start with the business problem: the work that is falling behind, your existing management capacity and the hours you need covered. The team can confirm whether its current staffing offer fits your requirements.

FAQ

What does a medical billing staffing agency do?

A staffing agency recruits billing personnel for an agreed role. In a dedicated remote staffing arrangement, the people work in your systems and your manager directs the queues. Ask which recruiting, employment, supervision, equipment and replacement responsibilities the written agreement includes.

How is a staffing agency different from a medical billing company?

A staffing arrangement supplies people for work you manage. A full-service billing company takes responsibility for an agreed billing function and directs its delivery. Compare responsibility, reporting and contract scope before comparing prices.

How much does a medical billing staffing agency charge?

Request a written quote for the role, hours and service scope. Compare the agency fee plus software, secure access, onboarding and your manager's time. A staff member's wage, an agency's client fee and a percentage-of-collections billing fee are different measures.

Can I hire help for one billing queue?

You can define a role around a specific queue, such as A/R follow-up or eligibility verification. Confirm minimum engagement, available schedules and relevant experience with the provider before deciding whether that arrangement fits.
