# Medical billing outsourcing statistics: who outsources what, and what it costs when billing breaks

*By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026 · [How we research and review](/about/#how-we-write-our-guides)*

**Short answer:** In a Guidehouse and HFMA survey of 191 provider executives (late 2025), 69% said they outsource all or part of their revenue cycle, and 66.7% use a vendor for A/R follow-up, the most outsourced function ([Guidehouse and HFMA, 2026](https://guidehouse.com/-/media/new-library/industries/health/documents/2026/health-revenue-cycle-management-report2026327.pdf)). Experian Health's 2025 survey of 250 billing decision makers found 41% of providers face denial rates of 10% or higher and 43% report being understaffed ([Experian Health, Sept. 22, 2025](https://experianplc.com/newsroom/press-releases/2025/experian-health-s-3rd-annual-state-of-claims-survey-finds-denial)). Guidehouse sells RCM services, so its numbers are vendor-sponsored.

This page collects the public numbers we could trace to a named source, a date and a sample. Each figure sits next to its publisher and link. Where a source is a vendor, a self-published press release or an analyst estimate, we say so. For how the models compare in practice, see our guide to [medical billing outsourcing](/medical-billing-outsourcing/).

## Who outsources what: adoption by revenue cycle function

Guidehouse and HFMA published the 2026 Revenue Cycle Management Trends report. It is based on a survey of 191 provider executives in late 2025: 52% hospital, 39% health system and 9% medical group respondents, and 47% were CFOs. The report is a free download. Guidehouse is an RCM managed-services vendor, so treat it as vendor-sponsored.

## The counter-trend: work moving back in-house

Black Book Research surveyed "revenue cycle leaders from 220 hospitals and health systems" in Q1 2025 and published its results in a press release dated April 18, 2025. We read it through a [republication in European Business Magazine](https://europeanbusinessmagazine.com/ai-disrupts-traditional-rcm-outsourcing-as-health-systems-redefine-vendor-expectations-black-book-survey/). Caveat: this is a self-published press release. It gives no sampling method or margin of error, and Black Book sells vendor evaluations. The release also promotes AI-enabled RCM. Treat these as directional.

| Black Book finding (Q1 2025, 220 hospitals and health systems) | Share |
|---|---|
| Actively restructuring or reassessing outsourcing contracts | 79% |
| Plan to move previously outsourced functions in-house or to platforms within 24 months | 72% |
| Have already reduced dependence on BPO vendors for at least one RCM function | 64% |
| Will require embedded AI and RPA in new or renewed deals | 91% |
| Considering hybrid vendor models | 48% |
| Average cost-to-collect reduction at hospitals with AI RCM platforms (no sample or date given) | 18% to 28% |

These numbers describe large hospitals and health systems. They do not show what small practices are doing. Guidehouse's survey above shows outsourcing at 69% (or 67%), so the two sources describe a market that is both heavily outsourced and reconsidering its contracts.

## Staffing and turnover

- **Understaffing:** 43% of providers report being understaffed. Source: [Experian Health, State of Claims 2025](https://experianplc.com/newsroom/press-releases/2025/experian-health-s-3rd-annual-state-of-claims-survey-finds-denial), press release dated September 22, 2025, n=250 financial, billing and claims decision makers, surveyed June to July 2025.
- **Turnover, medical groups:** In an MGMA Stat poll (May 26, 2026, 303 applicable responses), 29% of medical groups reported higher staff turnover than last year, 39% the same and 30% lower ([MGMA Stat](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/stabilized-but-not-solved-staff-turnover-in-2026)). This covers all staff, not billing staff.
- **Turnover, 2025:** On the same page, MGMA says 70% reported turnover the same or lower versus 2024 in its May 2025 poll. The page does not state that sample.
- **One health system's view:** Centra Health said it has "a significant number of positions in revenue cycle that we struggle to fill" and uses offshore labor. This came from an [HFMA Executive Roundtable](https://www.hfma.org/revenue-cycle/revenue-cycle-staffing-challenges-persist-hospitals-turn-to-automation-outsourcing/) sponsored by Omega Healthcare (March 31, 2025, 10 panelists). It is an anecdote, not a survey.

We did not find billing-specific vacancy or turnover rates from a primary source. If you see a precise "billing staff turnover" figure elsewhere, ask where it came from. Our [revenue cycle management staffing guide](/revenue-cycle-management-staffing/) covers how practices plan around vacancies.

## Administrative cost and automation

The 2025 CAQH Index (released February 19, 2026) draws on "more than 600 provider organizations and health plans representing 63 percent of insured lives." We read it through the [CAQH press release now hosted at DataSpring](https://www.dataspring.com/blog/2025-caqh-index-shows-u.s.-healthcare-avoided-258-billion-and-accelerated-automation-interoperability-and-ai-adoption). We could not open the executive report, so we have no per-transaction costs.

- $258 billion in administrative costs avoided in 2024.
- A remaining $21 billion savings opportunity from full automation.
- 25% of provider organizations use AI tools in administrative workflows, as do more than half of health plans.

## Prior authorization workload

The American Medical Association surveyed 1,000 physicians in late 2024 and reported results on [AMA News Wire](https://ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/fixing-prior-auth-nearly-40-prior-authorizations-week-way) (April 24, 2025):

- Practices complete 39 prior authorization requests per physician, per week.
- Physicians and their staff spend an average of 13 hours a week on them.
- 40% of physicians have staff who work exclusively on prior authorizations.

Other AMA releases cite 43 to 45 requests a week and 35% for the same survey year. We have not reconciled them. If this work is eating your team, see our guide to the [prior authorization specialist](/prior-authorization-specialist/) role.

## How we built this page

Source types matter. Guidehouse (an RCM managed-services vendor), Black Book (a self-published research firm), Experian Health (a claims-software vendor), Premier (a group purchasing and consulting company) and Reanin (a market-research publisher) each have a commercial interest in the topic; KFF, the AMA, CAQH, MGMA and the BLS do not sell revenue cycle services. We say which is which where each figure appears.

We read every source named above directly on October 30, 2026. We did not take any number from a search snippet. For each figure we recorded the publisher, link, date, sample size where given, and whether the underlying report is paywalled.

We excluded widely quoted figures we could not trace to a primary source: Black Book's "97%" of providers outsourcing at least one function, MGMA's "32% turnover" and "68 days to fill," and Premier's "$57.23" cost per claim. They appear on vendor blogs, but we found no primary page behind them. We also did not find a verified cost to rework a single claim beyond the Premier figure above, or a primary-source benchmark for days in A/R.

## What the numbers mean for a practice

- **A/R follow-up is the most outsourced function.** 66.7% of surveyed providers use a vendor for it, more than any other function in the Guidehouse and HFMA survey. If aging claims are your problem, start with our guide to [reducing A/R days](/reduce-ar-days/).
- **Denial rework still needs people.** 90% of denials are reworked with at least some human review (Experian Health), and nearly 70% of denied claims are ultimately overturned (Premier). Under-worked denials can leave recoverable money on the table. See [denial management](/denial-management/).
- **Understaffing is common.** 43% of providers report it (Experian Health), and one health system said it struggles to fill revenue cycle roles. That is why many practices look at [remote medical coders](/hire-remote-medical-coders/) and [offshore medical billers](/offshore-medical-billers/).
- **Prior authorization takes real hours.** Physicians and their staff spend an average of 13 hours a week on it (AMA). Read about the [prior authorization specialist](/prior-authorization-specialist/) role.
- **Outsourcing is not one decision.** Adoption ranges from 66.7% for A/R follow-up to 7.8% for utilization management (inferred pairing) in a hospital-heavy sample. You can outsource one function and keep the rest. See our [A/R specialist](/ar-specialist/) guide.
- **Vendor and analyst numbers need care.** Several figures here come from vendors or self-published releases. Check the primary source before you quote them to a board or a client.

## FAQ

### What percentage of healthcare providers outsource medical billing?

Guidehouse and HFMA surveyed 191 provider executives in late 2025. The report's snapshot says 69% outsource all or part of their revenue cycle, while its body text says 67% work with a managed services organization. Guidehouse sells RCM managed services, so treat the survey as vendor-sponsored. The sample is hospitals, health systems and medical groups, not small practices.

### Which revenue cycle functions are outsourced most?

In the same Guidehouse and HFMA survey, 66.7% use a third-party vendor for A/R follow-up and collections, 50.0% for coding, 39.2% for denial management and appeals, and 29.4% for billing management and claims editing. Utilization management is lowest at 7.8% and patient access next at 8.8%, but those pairings are inferred from chart order.

### Are health systems bringing billing back in-house?

Black Book Research says 72% of the revenue cycle leaders it surveyed at 220 hospitals and health systems plan to move outsourced functions in-house or to platforms within 24 months. Black Book is a self-published press release with no stated method, and it sells vendor evaluations, so read it as a signal, not a measurement.

### What is the average claim denial rate?

There is no single average. Experian Health's 2025 survey of 250 billing decision makers found 41% of providers face denial rates of 10% or higher. Premier reports that nearly 15% of claims to private payers are initially denied, and KFF found ACA marketplace insurers denied 19% of in-network claims in 2024.

### How much does it cost to rework a denied claim?

A Premier survey of over 500 organizations, reported by TechTarget in March 2024, put the average cost to fight a denied claim at $43.84, and the article says labor cost is not included. We could not verify other widely quoted per-claim figures, so we do not use them.
