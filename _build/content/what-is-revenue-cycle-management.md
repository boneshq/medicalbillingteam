# What is revenue cycle management (RCM) in healthcare?

*By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published October 18, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 · [How we research and review](/about/#how-we-write-our-guides)*

**Short answer:** Revenue cycle management (RCM) is the set of administrative and clinical-support steps a healthcare provider uses to get paid for its services, from scheduling and insurance verification through coding, claims, payment posting, denial management and patient collections. Good RCM means clean claims go out quickly, payments are posted accurately, denials are worked and prevented, and nothing ages past filing or appeal limits.

Every patient visit starts a financial process that can take weeks or months to finish. Revenue cycle management is the name for managing that process end to end. This guide explains each step, the metrics used to measure RCM, common problems, how to improve it, and how practices staff it. For the staffing side in depth, see our companion pillar on [revenue cycle management staffing](/revenue-cycle-management-staffing/).

## The revenue cycle, step by step

### 1. Scheduling and patient registration
The cycle begins when a patient books. Front-desk staff, or a [virtual medical receptionist](/virtual-medical-receptionist/), capture demographics and insurance details. Errors here flow through to every later step.

### 2. Insurance eligibility and benefits verification
Staff confirm active coverage, plan type, cost sharing and coordination of benefits before the visit. See our guide to [insurance verification services](/eligibility-verification-prior-authorization/). For treatment centers, this is the [verification of benefits (VOB)](/treatment-center-vob/) by level of care.

### 3. Prior authorization and referrals
Services that require payer approval need it before they're performed. Physicians in an AMA survey reported 39 prior authorizations per physician per week ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/fixing-prior-auth-nearly-40-prior-authorizations-week-way)). See [prior authorization specialists and outsourcing](/prior-authorization-specialist/).

### 4. Provider credentialing and enrollment
Providers must be enrolled with each payer, with confirmed effective dates, or their claims deny. See [medical credentialing services](/credentialing-services/).

### 5. Documentation and coding
Clinical documentation is translated into diagnosis (ICD-10-CM) and procedure (CPT/HCPCS) codes. ICD-10 updates take effect October 1 each year ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/icd-10-codes)). See [outsourcing medical coding](/hire-remote-medical-coders/).

### 6. Charge entry
Coded services are entered into the practice management system with the right fees, modifiers and provider information.

### 7. Claim submission and scrubbing
Claims are checked for errors and submitted to payers, usually through a clearinghouse. Rejections (claims not accepted for processing) are corrected and resubmitted quickly.

### 8. Payment posting
Payments from electronic remittance advice (ERAs) and EOBs are posted, reconciled to deposits, and checked against contracted rates. Remittances explain adjustments using claim adjustment reason codes (CARCs) from X12 ([X12](https://x12.org/codes/claim-adjustment-reason-codes)). See our [denial code guides](/denial-management/#denial-code-guides).

### 9. Denial management
Denied claims are categorized, corrected, resubmitted or appealed, and root causes are fed back upstream. KFF found HealthCare.gov insurers denied 20% of in-network claims in 2023 ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2023/)). See [denial management](/denial-management/).

### 10. Accounts receivable follow-up
Unpaid and underpaid claims are worked by age and dollar value until resolved. Medicare's claim filing limit is one calendar year from the date of service ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/manuals/downloads/clm104c01.pdf)). See [what an AR specialist does](/ar-specialist/) and [days in AR](/reduce-ar-days/).

### 11. Patient billing and collections
Patient balances (deductibles, coinsurance, self-pay) are billed, explained and collected, with payment plans where appropriate.

### 12. Reporting and improvement
Leaders review KPIs monthly and fix the causes of problems, not just the symptoms.

## Front end, middle and back end

People often group the steps into three parts:

| Part | Steps | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Front end | Scheduling, registration, eligibility, prior authorization, credentialing | Wrong insurance, missing authorizations, unenrolled providers |
| Middle | Documentation, coding, charge entry | Coding errors, missing modifiers, charge lag |
| Back end | Claims, posting, denials, A/R, patient collections | Unworked denials, aging A/R, underpayments |

Most back-end problems start at the front end, which is why denial work should always feed causes back upstream.

## Key RCM metrics

- **Days in A/R:** how long, on average, it takes to collect.
- **Clean claim rate:** claims accepted on first submission.
- **Denial rate:** denied claims as a share of claims submitted.
- **Net collection rate:** payments as a share of what you're contractually owed.
- **First-pass resolution:** claims paid or resolved on first submission.
- **A/R over 90 days:** the share of receivables at risk.

Benchmarks vary by specialty, payer mix and method, so track your own trend month over month. Definitions and formulas are in our [RCM staffing guide](/revenue-cycle-management-staffing/) and the [billing audit checklist](/checklist/).

## Why revenue cycle management matters

Payers set filing and appeal deadlines, administrative work keeps growing, and denials go unpaid if nobody works them. KFF found consumers appealed fewer than 1% of denied in-network claims in HealthCare.gov plans in 2023 ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2023/)). That's patient-side data, but the lesson carries over: denials that no one works don't get paid. Good RCM turns more of the care you provide into collected revenue, with less rework.

## How to improve revenue cycle management

1. **Verify eligibility and benefits before every visit,** not just for new patients.
2. **Track authorizations** with expirations and visit limits.
3. **Keep credentialing current** and tell billing about effective dates.
4. **Cut charge lag:** enter charges within a day or two of service.
5. **Scrub claims and work rejections daily.**
6. **Post payments daily** and flag zero-pay and underpaid lines.
7. **Own the denial queue,** sorted by payer and reason, worked weekly at minimum.
8. **Work A/R by dollar value and age,** with a documented next step on every claim.
9. **Review KPIs monthly** and fix one root cause at a time.
10. **Make sure each queue has enough people.** Many of these steps fail simply because no one has the time.

## Who does RCM? In-house, outsourced or dedicated staff

- **In-house team:** full control; you carry hiring, training, turnover and coverage. BLS reports a median wage of $23.32 an hour for billing and posting clerks (May 2025) ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ocwage.t01.htm)), before benefits; our [cost by state](/medical-billing-staffing-cost-by-state/) page shows the loaded figure for each state.
- **RCM company (outsourced RCM services):** runs most or all of the cycle, often for a percentage of collections. See [medical billing outsourcing](/medical-billing-outsourcing/).
- **Dedicated remote staff:** people who work only on your account, in your systems, under your direction. See [offshore medical billing](/offshore-medical-billers/) and [hiring a medical billing virtual assistant](/hire-remote-medical-billers/).

## RCM and HIPAA

Everyone in the revenue cycle handles protected health information. Any outside company whose staff access PHI on your behalf is generally a business associate and needs a business associate agreement before access ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html)). There is no official "HIPAA certification." See our [HIPAA guide](/hipaa-outsourced-medical-billing/).

## FAQ

### What is revenue cycle management in healthcare?

The set of steps a provider uses to get paid for care, from scheduling and insurance verification through coding, claims, payment posting, denial management, A/R follow-up and patient collections.

### What are the main steps of the revenue cycle?

Scheduling and registration, eligibility verification, prior authorization, credentialing, documentation and coding, charge entry, claim submission, payment posting, denial management, A/R follow-up, patient collections and reporting.

### When does the revenue cycle begin?

When the patient schedules. Registration and insurance details captured then affect every later step.

### How can a practice improve revenue cycle management?

Verify eligibility before every visit, track authorizations, keep credentialing current, reduce charge lag, work rejections and denials on a schedule, post payments daily, follow up on A/R by value and age, and review KPIs monthly.

### What is the difference between RCM and medical billing?

Medical billing usually means creating, submitting and following up on claims. RCM is broader and includes front-end steps like eligibility, authorization and credentialing, plus reporting and process improvement.
