# VOB for drug and alcohol treatment centers: verification of benefits done right

*By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published October 18, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 · [How we research and review](/about/#how-we-write-our-guides)*

**Short answer:** A verification of benefits (VOB) for a substance abuse treatment center confirms, before admission, what a prospective patient's plan covers for each level of care you offer: detox, residential, partial hospitalization, intensive outpatient and outpatient. That means in- and out-of-network benefits, deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums, authorization and pre-certification rules, day limits and whether behavioral health is carved out. Admissions needs the answer fast and accurate, and every VOB should be documented so billing and utilization review can rely on it.

In treatment centers, the revenue cycle starts on the admissions phone call. A fast, accurate verification of benefits decides whether admissions can give a family a straight answer, whether the program gets paid for the days it provides, and how much billing has to clean up later. This guide covers what a good VOB includes, where it goes wrong, and how centers staff it. It's part of our [behavioral health billing](/behavioral-health-billing/) guide.

## What to verify, by level of care

For each level of care you offer, confirm and record:

- active coverage, plan type and policy dates;
- **in-network and out-of-network benefits** (many treatment centers work out of network, so OON benefits matter);
- deductible, coinsurance, copays and out-of-pocket maximum, plus amounts met to date;
- **authorization and pre-certification requirements**, including who to call and what clinical information is needed;
- day or visit limits and step-down rules;
- whether behavioral health is **carved out** to a separate administrator with a different phone number, portal and payer ID;
- facility vs professional benefits, and any exclusions (for example, for certain residential settings); and
- the payer representative's name, the call reference number, and date and time.

Levels of care commonly verified: detox (withdrawal management), residential, partial hospitalization (PHP), intensive outpatient (IOP) and outpatient. Ask about each one separately; benefits often differ.

## Where VOBs go wrong

- **Stopping at "active coverage."** The plan may be active but carve behavioral health out, exclude a level of care or require pre-certification.
- **Wrong entity.** Calling the medical plan when behavioral health is administered elsewhere.
- **Undocumented calls.** Without a reference number and representative name, there's nothing to point to when the claim is later denied.
- **Speed vs accuracy.** Admissions wants an answer while the family is on the phone. A VOB checklist by payer keeps speed from turning into guesswork.
- **No handoff.** The authorization requirements found during the VOB must reach utilization review before the first day, or days go unpaid.

Denials tied to eligibility and coordination of benefits are common across healthcare. In a 2025 vendor survey of billing professionals, coordination of benefits was the most commonly cited denial reason (45% of respondents) ([Tebra](https://www.tebra.com/theintake/getting-paid/medical-billing-pain-points-insights-solutions)). See the [CO-22 coordination of benefits guide](/denial-codes/co-22/) and the [CO-197 authorization guide](/denial-codes/co-197/).

## After the VOB: authorizations and concurrent review

Higher levels of care usually require pre-certification and **concurrent review**, meaning periodic clinical updates to keep authorized days. The VOB tells you the rules; utilization review staff then track authorization numbers, approved days and the next review date. Missed reviews mean unpaid days. See our [prior authorization guide](/prior-authorization-specialist/).

## Privacy: 42 CFR Part 2

Records of federally assisted substance use disorder programs are protected by 42 CFR Part 2 in addition to HIPAA. HHS notes that compliance with the 2024 final rule was required by February 16, 2026, and that some Part 2 requirements also apply to recipients of Part 2 records, such as covered entities and business associates ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/hipaa-part-2/index.html)). Anyone doing VOBs, admissions or billing for a Part 2 program needs training on these rules. For legal questions, talk to counsel.

## Staffing VOB and admissions support

VOB work is phone- and portal-based, which makes it well suited to dedicated remote staff, including evening and weekend coverage if you schedule it. What to look for:

- experience with behavioral health payers, carve-outs and out-of-network benefits;
- a written VOB checklist by payer and level of care;
- fast, documented turnaround that admissions can rely on;
- a clean handoff to utilization review and billing; and
- training on HIPAA and Part 2, with a BAA in place before any access ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html)).

## FAQ

### What is a VOB in addiction treatment?

A verification of benefits confirms a prospective patient's coverage for each level of care, including in- and out-of-network benefits, cost sharing, authorization rules, day limits and carve-outs, usually before admission.

### What levels of care should a VOB cover?

Each level the program offers, commonly detox (withdrawal management), residential, partial hospitalization, intensive outpatient and outpatient. Benefits often differ by level.

### Why do treatment center VOBs go wrong?

Common causes are stopping at active coverage, calling the wrong entity when behavioral health is carved out, not documenting the call, and not handing authorization requirements to utilization review.

### Does 42 CFR Part 2 apply to VOB staff?

Staff who handle records of a federally assisted substance use disorder program must follow Part 2 as well as HIPAA. HHS says compliance with the 2024 final rule was required by February 16, 2026.

### Can VOBs be done remotely?

Yes. VOB work is phone- and portal-based. Remote staff need training on behavioral health benefits, HIPAA and Part 2, a BAA, and secure access.
