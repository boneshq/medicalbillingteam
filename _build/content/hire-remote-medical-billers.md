# Hiring remote and offshore medical billers: what to look for

*By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published October 18, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 · [How we research and review](/about/#how-we-write-our-guides)*

**Short answer:** To hire a medical billing virtual assistant or other remote biller, define the queue first and test candidates with a practical exercise on fictional data. Then choose between a freelancer, where you handle vetting and security, and an agency, which handles employment, equipment, backup coverage and the BAA. Before anyone logs in, require a BAA, MFA, secure remote access and role-based access.

Remote medical billers can add real capacity, or create new problems, depending on how you hire. This guide covers the roles you can fill, how to choose between a freelancer and an agency, what skills to test, the security practices to ask about, and how to onboard. It's written for practices and for billing companies adding staff. It's part of our [revenue cycle management staffing](/revenue-cycle-management-staffing/) series.

## Why practices and billing companies hire remotely

- **Hiring difficulty.** In a 2025 vendor survey, 38% of billing professionals said they struggle to find qualified candidates ([Tebra](https://www.tebra.com/theintake/getting-paid/medical-billing-pain-points-insights-solutions)).
- **Remote work is already normal in billing.** In the same survey, nearly 48% of respondents said they had implemented remote work arrangements ([Tebra](https://www.tebra.com/theintake/getting-paid/medical-billing-pain-points-insights-solutions)).
- **Coverage.** A second person who knows your payers keeps queues moving when someone is out.
- **Cost structure.** Remote and offshore staff can cost less per hour than local hires, but compare total cost, as our [cost guide](/medical-billing-staffing-cost/) explains.

## Roles you can hire remotely

- Billing / claims specialist
- A/R follow-up specialist
- Denial management specialist
- Eligibility and benefits verification (VOB) specialist
- Prior authorization coordinator
- Payment poster
- Medical coder (with credentials)
- Credentialing specialist
- Admissions / intake support (common in treatment centers)

Start with one well-defined queue. Eligibility, payment posting and A/R follow-up are common first roles because the work is measurable.

## Freelancer, agency or billing company?

| | Freelancer | Staffing agency | Billing company |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who directs the work | You | You | Vendor |
| Recruiting/vetting | You | Agency | Vendor |
| Backup coverage | Rarely | Usually (confirm) | Vendor |
| Equipment/security | Freelancer's own | Agency-managed (confirm) | Vendor |
| BAA | Must sign one | Agency signs | Vendor signs |
| Cost basis | Hourly | Monthly/hourly per FTE | % of collections or per claim |

Freelancers can work for small needs, but you carry the vetting, continuity and security risk. Agencies cost more than a freelancer's rate but handle employment, equipment, backfill and the business associate relationship. ## Skills to look for

**Core billing skills**
- Experience in your practice management system, or a comparable one
- Payer portals and clearinghouse workflows
- Reading ERAs (835s) and EOBs; understanding CARC and RARC codes
- Claim status, corrected claims and appeals
- Timely filing awareness by payer

**Specialty knowledge** (if relevant)
- Behavioral health levels of care and authorizations ([behavioral health guide](/behavioral-health-billing/))
- Therapy visit limits and modifiers
- Lab or ASC billing rules

**Work habits**
- Clear written and spoken English for payer calls and internal notes
- Documentation of every action
- Comfort with queues, targets and follow-up dates
- Knowing when to escalate

**For coders:** recognized coding credentials and specialty experience, verified directly with the credentialing body.

## How to assess candidates

1. **Structured interview** with the same questions for every candidate.
2. **Practical test using fictional or de-identified data.** Never use real patient information in a test. Examples:
   - Interpret a sample remittance and identify denied lines and next steps.
   - Decide how to handle a coordination-of-benefits denial.
   - Write a payer call note.
   - Calculate days in A/R from sample numbers.
3. **Reference checks** on billing-specific work.
4. **Schedule check:** confirm hours overlap with payer phone lines and your team.

### Sample interview questions

- Walk me through how you work an A/R queue on a Monday morning.
- A claim was denied for no authorization, but you see one in the system. What do you do?
- How do you track timely filing limits across payers?
- What do you document after a payer call?
- Tell me about a time you found an underpayment.
- How do you handle a task you're not sure is within your authority?

## Security practices to ask about

Remote billers see protected health information all day. Before anyone logs in, ask the agency (or require of a freelancer):

- **Business associate agreement** signed before any PHI access ([HHS on business associates](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html))
- **Unique logins and multi-factor authentication** on every system
- **Secure remote access** via virtual desktop (VDI) or VPN
- **No local downloads, printing or USB storage**
- **Company-managed, encrypted devices** with current patches
- **Role-based, minimum necessary access**, reviewed regularly and removed the day someone leaves
- **HIPAA training** at onboarding and annually, with records
- **Background checks**
- **Current risk analysis** and incident response plan with a defined notification timeline
- **Work location controls:** office security or home-workspace privacy rules

And be wary of "HIPAA certified" claims. There is no official HIPAA certification; HHS's Office for Civil Rights doesn't certify vendors ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html)). Our full [HIPAA guide](/hipaa-outsourced-medical-billing/) includes a 15-question checklist.

### Offshore-specific checks

- Confirm payer, Medicaid and government contracts don't restrict offshore access to member data.
- Know which legal entity signs the BAA and where it's based.
- Ask about power and internet redundancy.
- Include offshore access in your own HIPAA risk analysis.

## Onboarding: the first 30 days

**Week 1:** BAA and agreements signed; accounts created with role-based access and MFA; secure access tested; HIPAA and policy training; system walkthroughs.

**Week 2:** Shadow and practice on a limited queue; daily review of every action; payer-specific notes started.

**Week 3:** Own a defined queue with daily spot checks; agree on productivity and quality measures.

**Week 4:** Review results; adjust targets; document procedures the new person helped clarify; decide whether to expand scope.

## Managing remote billers well

- A daily or weekly check-in with clear priorities
- Shared queue reports so work is visible
- Written escalation rules (write-offs, refunds and appeals need approval)
- Monthly KPI review (see [RCM KPIs](/revenue-cycle-management-staffing/))
- Quick feedback on errors, so they're fixed once

## FAQ

### Should I hire a freelance medical biller or go through an agency?

A freelancer can work for small, simple needs but leaves you handling vetting, backup coverage, equipment and security. An agency typically handles recruiting, employment, equipment, backfill and HIPAA arrangements such as a BAA, but adds a margin. Compare total cost and risk, not just the hourly rate.

### What skills should a remote medical biller have?

Experience with your practice management system or similar, payer portals, reading ERAs and EOBs, CARC/RARC codes, claim follow-up, clear written English and documentation habits. Specialty experience matters for behavioral health, therapy, labs and ASCs.

### How do I test a medical billing candidate?

Use a short practical test with de-identified or fictional data: interpret a remittance, decide next steps on a denied claim, draft a payer call note, or calculate days in A/R from sample figures. Never use real patient data in a test.

### What security practices should I ask an agency about?

A signed BAA, unique logins with MFA, secure remote access through VDI or VPN, no local downloads or printing, company-managed encrypted devices, role-based access, HIPAA training records, background checks, a current risk analysis and an incident response plan.

### Can offshore medical billers work US payer hours?

Many offshore teams work schedules that overlap US business hours. Confirm the schedule in writing, because payer calls and same-day eligibility work depend on it.
