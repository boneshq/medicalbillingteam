# Medical billing outsourcing: three models and how to choose

*By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published October 18, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 · [How we research and review](/about/#how-we-write-our-guides)*

**Short answer:** There are three main ways to outsource medical billing. A full-service billing company runs the whole function, usually for a percentage of collections. A staffing model gives you dedicated remote billers who work in your systems under your direction. A hybrid outsources specific queues, such as A/R or prior authorization. The right choice depends on how much control you want, your volume and your specialty.

"Outsourcing medical billing" can mean very different arrangements. Comparing a percentage-of-collections billing company with a dedicated remote biller is like comparing a managed service with a hire. This guide lays out the models so you can compare them fairly. It's part of our [revenue cycle management staffing](/revenue-cycle-management-staffing/) series.

## Model 1: Full-service billing company

The billing company takes over most or all of the revenue cycle: charge entry, claims, payment posting, A/R, denials and often patient statements. It usually works in its own software or yours.

- **Typical pricing:** a percentage of collections, a per-claim fee or a flat monthly fee
- **Good fit:** practices that want billing off their plate entirely
- **Watch for:** less visibility into day-to-day work, contract terms that make leaving hard, unclear ownership of data and reports, and fees on collections the practice would have received anyway

## Model 2: Dedicated remote staff (staffing model)

A staffing provider recruits and employs billers who work only on your account, in your systems, following your procedures. You direct the work; the provider handles employment, equipment, backup coverage and HR.

- **Typical pricing:** a monthly or hourly rate per full-time person
- **Good fit:** practices and billing companies that want control and visibility, and have someone to direct the work
- **Watch for:** you still need management time and documented procedures; quality depends on hiring and training

## Model 3: Hybrid

Keep billing in-house and outsource specific queues: A/R follow-up, denials, eligibility, prior authorization or credentialing. This works well when one area is chronically behind.

- **Typical pricing:** per person, per task or per queue
- **Good fit:** practices with a capable in-house biller who is overloaded
- **Watch for:** clear handoffs between teams so nothing falls through the gaps

## How to compare the models

| Factor | Billing company | Dedicated remote staff | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who directs the work | Vendor | You | Both |
| Software | Vendor's or yours | Yours | Yours |
| Pricing basis | % of collections or per claim | Per person | Per person or task |
| Visibility | Vendor reports | Your queues and reports | Mixed |
| Management time needed | Low | Medium | Medium |
| Exit difficulty | Contract-dependent | Usually simpler | Usually simpler |

## Questions to ask any outsourcing partner

1. Who exactly will work on our account, and where are they located?
2. Will you sign a business associate agreement, and do your subcontractors sign one too?
3. What access controls do you use (MFA, secure remote access, no local downloads)?
4. What reports will we get, and can we see the work queues directly?
5. How are errors, write-offs and refunds approved?
6. What happens to our data and reports if we end the relationship?
7. What are the contract term, notice period and fees for leaving?
8. Can you give references we can contact directly?

Be cautious of anyone who promises a specific reduction in denials or A/R days before reviewing your data. Results depend on your payers, specialty and processes.

## HIPAA applies to every model

Any outsourcing partner whose people access patient information on your behalf is generally a business associate under HIPAA, and you need a BAA before access ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html)). There is no official "HIPAA certification" for vendors. Ask about specific safeguards instead, using our [HIPAA guide](/hipaa-outsourced-medical-billing/). If the team is overseas, read our guide to [offshore medical billers](/offshore-medical-billers/).

## Which model fits?

- **Small practice, no billing manager, wants it handled:** a billing company is often simplest.
- **Practice or billing company with a manager and growing volume:** dedicated remote staff gives capacity with control.
- **Strong in-house biller, one area behind:** hybrid.

## FAQ

### What does it cost to outsource medical billing?

Billing companies commonly charge a percentage of collections, per claim or a flat fee. Dedicated remote staff are usually priced per full-time person. Compare total cost, including your management time, with a worksheet.

### Is outsourcing medical billing HIPAA compliant?

It can be, when the partner signs a BAA and uses appropriate safeguards. HIPAA compliance depends on contracts and practices, not a certification; there is no official HIPAA certification.

### What is the difference between a billing company and a staffing model?

A billing company runs the function and directs the work. With a staffing model, dedicated remote billers work in your systems under your direction.

### Can I outsource only part of my billing?

Yes. A hybrid model outsources specific queues such as A/R follow-up, denials, eligibility, prior authorization or credentialing.

### Will outsourcing reduce my denials?

It depends on your payers, specialty, documentation and processes. Be cautious of promises of a specific reduction made before anyone reviews your data.
