# Medical billing RFP template and vendor evaluation scorecard

*By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published November 7, 2026 · Updated November 7, 2026 · [How we research and review](/about/#how-we-write-our-guides)*

**Short answer:** A useful medical billing request for proposal makes vendors price the same work and show evidence for their answers. Start with a one-page scope, ask the 20 questions below, resolve mandatory requirements, then compare the remaining proposals with the 100-point scorecard. Copy these tables into your own document; no signup is required.

This is an original procurement worksheet from Medical Billing Team, operated by Alliance Global Solutions. The weights are suggested editorial choices, not industry benchmarks. Use the same process when evaluating AGS. The worksheet supports purchasing discussions; your legal and compliance advisers should review the actual agreements.

## 1. Copy this scope brief

Fill in business-level information. Approximate or aggregate volumes are enough for an initial conversation. Do not put patient names, insurance IDs, claim images or other patient information in a general RFP or website form.

| Field | Your requirement |
|---|---|
| Organization and contact | Business name, contact name and work email |
| Service model | Dedicated staff, managed billing service, or a defined project |
| Goal | The specific queue, coverage gap or operating problem to address |
| Work included | For example: unpaid insurance claim follow-up and next-action notes |
| Work excluded | For example: coding changes, refunds and write-offs without approval |
| Specialty and payer mix | Specialty and aggregate payer categories; no patient-level details |
| Workload | Incoming items per week or month, existing backlog and age bands |
| Systems | Practice management system, clearinghouse and required portals |
| Coverage | Time zone, hours, languages and holiday requirements |
| Ownership | Your supervisor, vendor contact and exception approver |
| Reporting | Measures, definitions, reporting frequency and access to underlying reports |
| Timeline | Proposal deadline, decision date and proposed onboarding period |
| Commercial assumptions | Hours or workload priced, minimum term and requested fee breakdown |

If the service model is undecided, start with the [outsourcing models comparison](/medical-billing-outsourcing/) and [staffing agency guide](/medical-billing-staffing-agency/). Ask vendors to price alternatives separately so the scope remains visible.

## 2. Ask these 20 questions

Request a written answer and the named evidence for each question. Record exceptions rather than treating a sales presentation as a completed response.

### Scope, people and supervision

1. **Which tasks are included and excluded?** Attach a responsibility matrix naming the vendor and client owners.
2. **Who will perform the work, and from where?** Identify the contracting entity, staff locations and any subcontractors.
3. **How will you demonstrate relevant experience?** Describe work on our specialty, payer types and systems; provide a fictional work sample or assessment plan.
4. **Who manages daily priorities and quality?** Name the supervisory role and provide a sample review schedule.
5. **What happens during leave, turnover or volume spikes?** Explain coverage, replacement terms, training responsibility and any extra charges.

### Access, safeguards and contract requirements

6. **What agreements apply before anyone accesses protected information?** Supply the proposed BAA and identify subcontractor arrangements for review.
7. **How are accounts created, restricted and removed?** Walk through approval, individual credentials, access review and offboarding.
8. **How is remote work secured?** Explain device management, authentication, downloads, printing, physical workspace and logging.
9. **What happens after a security incident?** Identify contacts, escalation steps and the proposed contractual reporting timeline.
10. **Which software or AI tools would touch our information?** List tools, their purpose, who operates them and the approval process for changes.

HHS's [business associate contract guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/sample-business-associate-agreement-provisions/index.html) explains agreement provisions concerning permitted uses, safeguards, reporting, subcontractors and return or destruction of information when feasible. A questionnaire or high score does not establish compliance. Review proposed contracts and controls with your adviser. For more discussion, use our [outsourced billing HIPAA guide](/hipaa-outsourced-medical-billing/).

### Workflow and measurable performance

11. **How do you prioritize work nearing a deadline?** Show how payer-specific filing and appeal dates are recorded, checked and escalated.
12. **How do you distinguish rejections, denials and unpaid claims?** Provide a sample status report and the definitions used.
13. **What counts as a completed task?** Explain whether an item was reviewed, corrected, submitted, followed up or paid.
14. **How is quality measured?** Describe sample selection, error categories, reviewer responsibility and correction tracking.
15. **Can we reconcile your reports to our systems?** Show a fictional example with a reporting period, source and denominator for each percentage.

Deadlines need their own workflow. For example, Medicare generally requires filing with the correct Medicare Administrative Contractor within one calendar year of service; exceptions and other payer rules require separate review. A vendor should demonstrate how it keeps the applicable rules current. [CMS timely filing guidance](https://www.cms.gov/Outreach-and-Education/MLN/WBT/MLN4462429-MLN-WBT-1500/1500/lesson01/08/index.html).

### Price, onboarding and exit

16. **What is the exact fee basis?** State per-person, hourly, per-claim, percentage-of-collections or other terms, and define the billable unit.
17. **What costs are additional?** Itemize software, implementation, equipment, overtime, coverage, management and any minimum charges.
18. **What does onboarding require from us?** Supply a proposed schedule with access, training and acceptance checkpoints.
19. **How does the engagement end?** Describe notice, fees, data export, open-work handoff, access removal and information handling.
20. **What can we independently verify?** Offer relevant references with permission and identify which claims are supported by documents, demonstrations or contractual commitments.

## 3. Resolve mandatory requirements before scoring

Set these requirements before opening proposals. The exact requirements depend on your organization; these are examples to adapt with the people responsible for compliance and operations.

| Requirement | Decision to record |
|---|---|
| Necessary agreements and permitted staff locations | Accepted, needs clarification, or cannot proceed |
| Approved access and security arrangement | Accepted, needs clarification, or cannot proceed |
| Required schedule and essential scope | Accepted, needs clarification, or cannot proceed |
| Reporting access and workable exit terms | Accepted, needs clarification, or cannot proceed |

Resolve a material gap before award. A lower price or stronger interview should not offset a requirement your organization has made mandatory.

## 4. Use this 100-point vendor scorecard

**Method:** Rate each category from 0 to 5, then calculate **weighted points = category weight × rating ÷ 5**. Add the weighted points for a total out of 100. Choose your weights before scoring; if you change them, rescore every vendor consistently. Retain the evidence, reviewer and date beside each rating.

| Category | Suggested weight | Evidence to compare |
|---|---|---|
| Role, specialty and payer fit | 20 | Relevant work examples, candidate assessment and scope exceptions |
| Safeguards and access process | 20 | Reviewed documentation and a demonstrated access lifecycle |
| Workflow and quality control | 20 | Queue rules, escalation examples and a quality review method |
| Reporting and visibility | 15 | Reconciled sample report with definitions and source systems |
| Total cost and commercial clarity | 15 | Itemized like-for-like quote and understandable contract terms |
| Onboarding, continuity and exit | 10 | Named owners, training plan, coverage commitments and handoff process |
| **Total** | **100** | Apply the same evidence standard to every proposal |

**Rating anchors:** 0 = no usable evidence; 1 = assertion only; 2 = partial documentation with material gaps; 3 = documented approach meeting the stated need; 4 = approach supported by a relevant demonstration or corroboration; 5 = verified fit with clear commitments and no material unresolved gap in that category. These are procurement judgments, not certifications.

**Worked hypothetical example:** A vendor earns 4 out of 5 for role fit, weighted at 20. Its points for that category are 20 × 4 ÷ 5 = **16**. Repeat for all six categories. This example does not rate AGS or any other company.

For the cost category, normalize the quotes first with our [staffing cost worksheet](/medical-billing-staffing-cost/) and [state cost calculator](/medical-billing-staffing-cost-by-state/). Compare staff capacity and management obligations as well as price.

## 5. Agree the pilot reporting worksheet

Select measures that match the assigned work. This worksheet proposes definitions to agree with the vendor; it does not prescribe universal targets or promise collections results.

| Measure | Definition to agree | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Starting and ending queue | Same queue, period and included statuses; reconcile arrivals and exits | Separates backlog movement from new work |
| Completed actions | Count by action type, with rules for repeat touches | Distinguishes meaningful work from repeated activity |
| Quality review | Reviewed actions, actions with errors and severity; record sample method | Makes the numerator and denominator inspectable |
| Blocked items | Reason, owner, next action and age of block | Reveals where the practice or payer must act |
| Deadline exposure | Items approaching the applicable verified deadline | Makes urgent work visible |
| Collections, if relevant | Payments attributed to the agreed cohort and period, with exclusions stated | Limits misleading before-and-after comparisons |

A small or selectively chosen quality sample can mislead. Record how the sample was chosen and avoid presenting its result as the error rate of the entire operation. Compare periods using consistent definitions, then discuss payer delays, documentation gaps and other factors outside the assigned team's control.

## FAQ

### What should a medical billing RFP include?

Include the service model, work scope, approximate volumes, systems, payer mix, coverage hours, responsibilities, security requirements, reporting definitions, pricing assumptions and exit terms. Ask vendors to answer the same questions and attach supporting evidence.

### Can I use this RFP for remote billing staff?

Yes. For staffing, specify the roles and hours you need, who directs the work, which tools you provide and how candidates will be assessed. For a managed billing service, define the functions and outcomes the vendor is responsible for delivering.

### How do I score medical billing vendors?

Resolve mandatory requirements first. Then rate each weighted category from zero to five based on documented evidence and multiply its weight by the rating divided by five. The suggested weights on this page total 100; they are an editorial starting point, not an industry standard or vendor ranking.

### Should I send patient records with an RFP?

Use aggregate business volumes and fictional work samples for initial proposals. Do not attach patient records or claim-level data to a general inquiry. Any later sharing of protected information needs an approved purpose, appropriate agreements and a secure channel.

### Is the lowest billing quote the best value?

Compare quotes only after matching scope, hours, fee basis and included costs. Add software, setup, management and exit costs. A low quote with an undefined queue, missing coverage or unclear reporting may not meet the same requirement as another proposal.
