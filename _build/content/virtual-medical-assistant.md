# Virtual medical assistants for billing and front-office work

*By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published October 18, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 · [How we research and review](/about/#how-we-write-our-guides)*

**Short answer:** A virtual medical assistant is a remote staff member who handles administrative work for a practice, such as eligibility checks, prior authorization follow-up, appointment reminders, data entry, payment posting and inbox management. A virtual assistant is not a clinical medical assistant and shouldn't do clinical tasks. Because the role touches patient data, require a BAA and secure access before day one.

"Virtual medical assistant" is a broad term. In practice it usually means remote administrative help for an overloaded front desk or billing team. This guide covers what a virtual assistant can and can't do, how the role fits with billing, and how to set it up safely. It's part of our [revenue cycle management staffing](/revenue-cycle-management-staffing/) series.

## What a virtual medical assistant can do

**Front-office and scheduling support**
- Appointment confirmations and reminder calls
- Insurance card and demographic updates in the practice management system
- Referral and records-request tracking
- Inbox, fax and portal message triage (non-clinical routing)

**Billing-adjacent work**
- Eligibility and benefits checks before visits (see [eligibility verification](/eligibility-verification-prior-authorization/))
- Prior authorization submissions and follow-up (see [prior authorization specialist](/prior-authorization-specialist/))
- Charge entry from structured encounter forms
- Payment posting support and simple reconciliation
- Patient statement questions and payment plan setup, following your scripts

**Practice admin**
- Credentialing paperwork and expiration tracking (see [credentialing specialist](/credentialing-specialist/))
- Report preparation and data cleanup

## What to keep in-house or with licensed staff

- Clinical tasks, triage, medical advice or anything that requires a license
- In-person work: check-in, collecting payments, rooming patients
- Decisions on write-offs, refunds and policy exceptions (the assistant can prepare them, but a manager approves)

Medical assistants in a clinic are a different occupation with clinical duties; a virtual administrative assistant isn't a substitute for that role.

## Why practices use virtual assistants

Administrative load is a major pressure on small practices. Prior authorization alone accounted for about 13 hours a week of physician and staff time in an AMA survey ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/fixing-prior-auth-nearly-40-prior-authorizations-week-way)). Hiring is also hard: in a 2025 vendor survey, 38% of billing professionals said they struggle to find qualified candidates ([Tebra](https://www.tebra.com/theintake/getting-paid/medical-billing-pain-points-insights-solutions)). A virtual assistant can take repeatable administrative tasks off the front desk so in-office staff can focus on patients.

## Virtual assistant or dedicated biller?

| If your biggest problem is... | Consider |
|---|---|
| Phones, reminders, eligibility and paperwork | A virtual medical assistant |
| Unworked A/R and denials | A dedicated [AR specialist](/ar-specialist/) |
| Claims going out late or with errors | A dedicated remote biller |
| All of the above | A small team with defined queues |

## HIPAA and security setup

A virtual assistant who sees schedules, insurance information or billing records handles PHI. Before day one:

- sign a BAA with the staffing provider or contractor ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html));
- create unique logins with MFA, and use secure remote access through a virtual desktop or VPN;
- grant only the access the tasks require (minimum necessary);
- block local downloads, printing and personal devices; and
- train on HIPAA and your policies, and keep records.

There's no official "HIPAA certified" virtual assistant. See our [HIPAA guide](/hipaa-outsourced-medical-billing/) for the safeguards to ask about.

## How to start

1. **List the tasks** that eat the most front-desk and billing time each week.
2. **Write simple procedures** for the first two or three tasks, with scripts for patient calls.
3. **Start with a defined schedule** that overlaps your office hours.
4. **Check in daily** for the first two weeks, then weekly.
5. **Measure:** tasks completed, eligibility checked before visits, PA turnaround, and errors caught.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Handing over everything at once.** Start with two or three tasks and add more as procedures settle.
- **No written procedures.** Assistants can only follow the process you've written down; undocumented "tribal knowledge" leads to errors.
- **Shared logins.** Every person needs their own account so access can be audited and removed.
- **No single point of contact.** Name one person in the office who answers the assistant's questions and reviews their work.

## FAQ

### What does a virtual medical assistant do?

Remote administrative work such as appointment reminders, eligibility checks, prior authorization follow-up, data and charge entry, payment posting support, and inbox and fax triage.

### Can a virtual medical assistant do clinical tasks?

No. Virtual administrative assistants shouldn't perform clinical tasks, triage or give medical advice. Keep those with licensed, in-person staff.

### Is a virtual medical assistant HIPAA compliant?

A virtual assistant can work within HIPAA when there's a BAA, secure access, minimum necessary permissions and training. There is no official HIPAA certification for assistants or companies.

### How is a virtual medical assistant different from a medical biller?

A virtual assistant handles broad administrative tasks; a biller focuses on claims, A/R and denials. Many small practices start with an assistant and add billing capacity later.

### How do I start with a virtual medical assistant?

List the most time-consuming administrative tasks, write procedures for two or three of them, agree on a schedule, sign a BAA, and check in daily for the first two weeks.
