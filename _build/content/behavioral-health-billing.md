# Medical billing for behavioral health practices and treatment centers

*By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published October 18, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 · [How we research and review](/about/#how-we-write-our-guides)*

**Short answer:** Behavioral health billing is harder than it looks. Practices and treatment centers deal with time-based psychotherapy codes, frequent authorizations and concurrent reviews, benefits that vary by level of care, carved-out behavioral health plans, and extra privacy rules for substance use disorder records under 42 CFR Part 2. Billing staff need training in all of these.

Behavioral health billing looks simple from the outside: a lot of the same few codes. In practice, it's one of the more demanding specialties to bill well. Authorizations and concurrent reviews are frequent, benefits vary by level of care, many plans carve behavioral health out to a separate administrator, and substance use disorder (SUD) records carry their own federal privacy rules. This guide covers what's different and how practices and treatment centers staff the work. It's part of our [revenue cycle management staffing](/revenue-cycle-management-staffing/) series.

## Who this applies to

- Outpatient mental health practices: psychiatrists, psychologists, therapists, counselors and group practices
- Substance use disorder treatment centers across levels of care
- Intensive outpatient (IOP) and partial hospitalization (PHP) programs
- Residential treatment and detox programs
- Billing companies serving these providers

## What makes behavioral health billing different

### 1. Levels of care and benefits

Treatment centers bill across levels of care, each with its own benefits, authorization rules and documentation expectations. A patient's plan may cover residential care differently from IOP, with day limits or step-down requirements.

### 2. Authorizations and concurrent review

Many behavioral health services require prior authorization, and higher levels of care often require **concurrent review**: periodic clinical updates to the payer to continue authorized days. Missed reviews mean unpaid days. Staff need a tracker with review dates, approved days and the clinical contact for each case.

### 3. Carve-outs

A patient's medical plan may route behavioral health claims and authorizations to a separate behavioral health administrator with a different portal, payer ID and rules. Eligibility checks must identify the right entity.

### 4. Time-based and add-on codes

Psychotherapy codes are typically time-based, with add-on codes for things like interactive complexity or crisis services. Documentation must support the time and the service. Use current CPT guidance from the AMA ([AMA CPT overview](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/cpt/cpt-overview-and-code-approval)) and payer policies; coders and billers should work from the current year's code set.

### 5. Privacy rules beyond HIPAA

- **Psychotherapy notes** receive special protection under the HIPAA Privacy Rule: most uses and disclosures require patient authorization ([HHS, Privacy Rule summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html)). Billing staff generally don't need them and shouldn't have access.
- **42 CFR Part 2** protects records of federally assisted SUD programs. It limits disclosures that would identify someone as having an SUD unless Part 2 permits it, allows a single patient consent for treatment, payment and health care operations, and restricts use of records in legal proceedings against patients. HHS notes the 2024 final rule's compliance date was February 16, 2026, and that some Part 2 requirements also apply to recipients such as HIPAA covered entities, business associates and qualified service organizations ([HHS, Part 2](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/hipaa-part-2/index.html)).

Make sure anyone touching Part 2 records, including billing staff, is trained on these rules and that your agreements cover them.

## The treatment center revenue cycle

### Admissions and VOB

The cycle starts before admission. VOB staff verify:

- active coverage and plan type;
- in- and out-of-network benefits by level of care;
- deductibles, coinsurance and out-of-pocket maximums;
- authorization and pre-certification requirements;
- day limits and step-down rules; and
- whether behavioral health is carved out.

Admissions staff need answers fast, often while a prospective patient is on the phone. Speed and accuracy both matter. See [eligibility verification and prior authorization](/eligibility-verification-prior-authorization/).

### Utilization review support

Clinical staff conduct reviews, but billing and UR coordinators track authorization numbers, approved days, next review dates and denials, and make sure authorization data flows onto claims.

### Claims, denials and A/R

Common problems include authorization mismatches (dates or units on the claim not matching the authorization), wrong payer (carve-out not identified), missing documentation for level-of-care billing and timely filing. Our guides on [denial management](/denial-management/) and [reducing A/R days](/reduce-ar-days/) apply directly.

### Payment posting and reconciliation

Out-of-network claims, single-case agreements and patient responsibility make posting more complex. Accurate posting shows whether payers paid what was agreed.

## Mental health parity

Federal mental health parity rules generally require that limits on mental health and substance use disorder benefits be no more restrictive than those on medical and surgical benefits. The parity regulations and their enforcement have changed in recent years, so check current guidance from the Departments of Labor, HHS and the Treasury. Billing staff don't enforce parity, but recognizing patterns of more restrictive treatment can inform appeals. For specific parity questions, consult counsel.

## Staffing behavioral health billing

### Roles

- **VOB / admissions support specialist**
- **Authorization and concurrent review coordinator**
- **Behavioral health biller / claims specialist**
- **A/R and denial follow-up specialist**
- **Payment poster**

### What to look for

- Experience with behavioral health payers, carve-outs and levels of care
- Comfort working with admissions under time pressure
- Accurate tracking habits for authorizations and review dates
- Training on HIPAA, psychotherapy notes and Part 2
- Clear documentation of every payer interaction

### In-house, outsourced or remote?

- **In-house** works well when admissions and billing need constant face-to-face coordination.
- **Outsourced billing companies** that specialize in behavioral health can take the whole function; confirm how they handle VOB speed and Part 2.
- **Dedicated remote staff** can cover VOB, authorizations, claims and A/R in your systems, including evening and weekend admissions coverage if scheduled. Put a BAA and access controls in place first, and limit access to what each role needs ([HIPAA guide](/hipaa-outsourced-medical-billing/)).

## Checklist for behavioral health billing leaders

- [ ] Authorization matrix by payer, level of care and service
- [ ] Concurrent review calendar with owner for each case
- [ ] Carve-out identification step in every eligibility check
- [ ] Psychotherapy notes excluded from billing staff access
- [ ] Part 2 consent tracking and staff training documented
- [ ] Authorization data validated against claims before submission
- [ ] Monthly denial review by payer and reason

A note on scope: this guide covers billing operations, not clinical or legal requirements. Licensure, program certification and state rules vary, so check them with your state agencies and counsel.

## FAQ

### Why is behavioral health billing different from other specialties?

Behavioral health often involves time-based psychotherapy codes, frequent authorizations and concurrent reviews, levels of care with different benefits, carved-out behavioral health plans, and extra privacy rules for substance use disorder records under 42 CFR Part 2.

### What is a VOB in a treatment center?

A verification of benefits confirms a prospective patient's coverage and benefits for specific levels of care, such as detox, residential, partial hospitalization or intensive outpatient, including authorization requirements, day limits and out-of-network benefits, usually before admission.

### Does 42 CFR Part 2 apply to billing staff?

Part 2 applies to federally assisted SUD programs and to certain recipients of Part 2 records, including HIPAA covered entities and business associates and qualified service organizations. Billing staff who handle Part 2 records must follow its consent and redisclosure rules. HHS says compliance with the 2024 final rule was required by February 16, 2026.

### Can remote staff handle admissions and VOB for a treatment center?

Yes, VOB and admissions support are phone- and portal-based and can be done remotely, provided staff are trained on levels of care, coverage rules and privacy requirements, and work under a BAA with appropriate access controls.

### What should a behavioral health practice look for in billing staff?

Experience with behavioral health codes and payers, authorization and concurrent review tracking, knowledge of carve-out plans, understanding of Part 2 and psychotherapy notes rules, and strong documentation habits.
