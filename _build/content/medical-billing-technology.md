# Technology in medical billing: AI, automation and what's changing

*By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published October 18, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 · [How we research and review](/about/#how-we-write-our-guides)*

**Short answer:** Technology is changing medical billing in five main places: AI that helps code, scrub claims and predict denials; automation for eligibility checks and claim status; electronic prior authorization, which CMS-0057-F requires impacted payers to support through APIs by January 1, 2027; payer-policy data used to prevent denials before care is delivered; and AI assistants that help staff draft appeals and work queues. People still have to own the work: someone has to check the output, handle exceptions and follow up with payers.

This hub explains, in plain language, where AI and automation fit in the revenue cycle, what rules are driving change, and what it means for staffing. The **Latest updates** section below is refreshed regularly from our weekly news scan. Every item is dated and linked to its source.

## Where technology fits in the revenue cycle

### AI coding assistance
Computer-assisted and AI coding tools suggest codes from clinical documentation. They can speed up coding, but coders still review suggestions against documentation and payer rules, especially for modifiers and bundling (see [CO-97](/denial-codes/co-97/)). Code sets change on a schedule; ICD-10 updates take effect October 1 each year ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/icd-10-codes)). See [outsource medical coding](/hire-remote-medical-coders/).

### Claim scrubbing and denial prediction
Scrubbers check claims for missing or invalid data before submission, which targets errors like those behind [CO-16](/denial-codes/co-16/). Newer tools use payer rules and past adjudication data to flag claims likely to deny. Large RCM vendors are building payer-policy intelligence into the front end so requirements are visible before care is delivered ([Fierce Healthcare, Oct 13, 2026](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/thoreau-backed-ensemble-penelope-health-partner-tackle-payer-denials-upstream)).

### Eligibility and benefits automation
Electronic eligibility checks return coverage and benefit details in seconds, and batch checks can run ahead of the schedule. They don't always show carve-outs, authorization rules or out-of-network details, which is why [insurance verification](/eligibility-verification-prior-authorization/) and [treatment center VOBs](/treatment-center-vob/) still often need a phone call.

### Electronic prior authorization and CMS-0057-F
The CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule (CMS-0057-F) requires impacted payers to make decisions within 72 hours for expedited and seven calendar days for standard requests, give specific denial reasons, and build prior authorization APIs by January 1, 2027 ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-and-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f)). The AHA advises hospitals and health systems to prepare for significant changes to PA workflows and payer interactions as those provisions take effect ([AHA, Oct 15, 2026](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-10-15-aha-launches-new-website-prior-authorization)). See [prior authorization specialists and outsourcing](/prior-authorization-specialist/).

### Credentialing and enrollment tools
Credentialing platforms track documents, expirations and application status, and Medicare enrollment runs through CMS's online PECOS system ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/enrollment-renewal/providers-suppliers/chain-ownership-system-pecos)). See [credentialing services](/credentialing-services/).

### AI assistants for billing teams
General-purpose and RCM-specific AI assistants can help staff summarize payer policies, draft appeal letters, organize call notes and prioritize A/R work. Use them only in tools your organization has approved for PHI, under a BAA where the vendor handles PHI, and with a person reviewing anything that goes to a payer or patient.

### Payer-side AI and program integrity
Payers and regulators use AI too. CMS says it's piloting AI to flag suspicious Medicaid billing patterns before payment, and a CMS official acknowledged that "a lot of things" about its AI-supported prior authorization pilot (WISeR) "are not working" ([Nextgov/FCW, Oct 15, 2026](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/cms-hopes-quadruple-provider-suspensions-over-next-six-months-official-says/416640/)). Accurate coding and documentation matter more as payer review becomes automated.

## What technology doesn't replace

- Someone who owns each queue and follows up until claims are resolved
- Judgment on exceptions, appeals, write-offs and patient conversations
- Payer phone calls when portals and APIs don't answer the question
- Human review of AI output before it reaches a payer or patient

## HIPAA and AI tools

Any vendor whose tool creates, receives, maintains or transmits PHI on your behalf is generally a business associate and needs a BAA ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html)). Check where data goes, whether it's used to train models, and who can access it. See our [HIPAA guide](/hipaa-outsourced-medical-billing/).

## FAQ

### How is AI used in medical billing?

Common uses include coding suggestions, claim scrubbing, denial prediction, eligibility automation, payer-policy lookups and AI assistants that help staff draft appeals and prioritize work. People still review the output and handle exceptions.

### What does CMS-0057-F require for prior authorization technology?

Impacted payers must build prior authorization APIs by January 1, 2027, and meet decision timeframes of 72 hours for expedited and seven calendar days for standard requests, with specific denial reasons.

### Will automation replace billing staff?

Automation reduces manual steps, but someone still owns each queue, follows up with payers, handles exceptions and reviews AI output before it reaches a payer or patient.

### Do AI tools need a BAA?

If the vendor's tool creates, receives, maintains or transmits PHI on your behalf, the vendor is generally a business associate and needs a BAA.
