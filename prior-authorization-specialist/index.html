Prior authorization specialist: what the job involves and how to staff it

Short answer: A prior authorization (PA) specialist gets payer approval before services that require it. The specialist checks requirements, gathers clinical documentation, submits requests, follows up, and tracks approvals, denials, expirations and visit limits. The workload is heavy: physicians in an AMA survey reported 39 PA requests per physician per week, and 40%% have staff who work exclusively on PA.

Prior authorization is one of the most time-consuming administrative tasks in a practice, and when it goes wrong it shows up as delayed care and denied claims. This guide covers what a PA specialist does, what changed under the CMS prior authorization rule, and how to staff the role. It's part of our revenue cycle management staffing series.

How big the PA workload is

In an AMA survey of 1,000 physicians conducted in late 2024, physicians reported completing an average of 39 prior authorizations per physician per week. They and their staff spent about 13 hours a week on PA, and 40% said they have staff who work exclusively on it (AMA). PA problems also drive denials: in a 2025 vendor survey of billing professionals, 31% cited prior authorization or referral issues as a common denial reason (Tebra).

What a PA specialist does

  1. Identifies requirements. Checks whether the service, drug or procedure needs authorization for the patient's specific plan.
  2. Gathers documentation. Collects the clinical notes, test results and codes the payer requires, working with clinical staff.
  3. Submits the request through the payer's portal, an electronic workflow, fax or phone.
  4. Follows up on pending requests and responds to requests for more information.
  5. Records the outcome: authorization number, approved units or visits, dates and any conditions.
  6. Handles denials: routes them for peer-to-peer review or appeal, with deadlines noted.
  7. Tracks expirations and limits so renewals go in before care is interrupted.

What changed: CMS-0057-F

The CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule (CMS-0057-F) applies to impacted payers, including Medicare Advantage organizations, state Medicaid and CHIP programs, and certain other plans. Under the rule, starting in 2026 these payers must send decisions within 72 hours for expedited requests and seven calendar days for standard requests, and must give a specific reason for denials. Payers must also build prior authorization APIs by January 1, 2027. The rule excludes drugs (CMS fact sheet).

Faster decisions help, but they don't remove the work of preparing complete requests, tracking them and acting on denials. Our eligibility verification and prior authorization guide covers the full front-end workflow.

Appeals are worth tracking

KFF data cited by the AMA shows that in 2023, Medicare Advantage enrollees or providers appealed only 11.7% of PA denials, but 81.7% of appeals were fully or partially overturned (AMA). A PA specialist who flags appealable denials quickly, with the deadline, gives your clinicians the chance to act.

Skills to hire for

  • Knowledge of payer PA rules for your specialty and main payers
  • Comfort reading clinical documentation and matching it to payer criteria
  • Payer portal experience and organized follow-up
  • Accurate tracking of authorization numbers, units and dates
  • Clear communication with clinicians, front desk and billing
  • Training on HIPAA and minimum necessary access, because PA involves clinical information

Measuring the role

  • Requests submitted, pending and resolved each week
  • Average time from order to submission
  • Approvals, denials and appeals, by payer
  • Claims denied for missing or mismatched authorization (should trend down)
  • Authorizations renewed before expiration

Staffing options

PA work happens in portals, on the phone and in your EHR, so it can be done remotely. Options include in-house staff, a billing company that includes PA, or dedicated remote specialists working your queue. For high-volume specialties and treatment centers, a dedicated PA person often frees front-desk and clinical time (see our behavioral health billing guide). Sign a BAA and set role-based access before any remote specialist sees clinical records (HIPAA guide).

FAQ

What does a prior authorization specialist do?

Checks whether services need payer approval, gathers documentation, submits requests, follows up, records approvals and tracks expirations, and routes denials for peer-to-peer review or appeal.

How many prior authorizations do practices handle?

Physicians in an AMA survey conducted in late 2024 reported an average of 39 prior authorizations per physician per week, with about 13 hours of physician and staff time spent on them.

What are the CMS-0057-F decision timeframes?

For impacted payers, decisions within 72 hours for expedited requests and seven calendar days for standard requests, plus a specific reason for denials. Drugs are excluded.

Can prior authorization be done remotely?

Yes. The work happens in payer portals, by phone and in the EHR. Remote staff need a BAA, secure access, role-based permissions and HIPAA training.

Are prior authorization denials worth appealing?

Often. KFF data cited by the AMA shows 81.7%% of Medicare Advantage PA denials that were appealed in 2023 were fully or partially overturned, though only 11.7%% were appealed.
