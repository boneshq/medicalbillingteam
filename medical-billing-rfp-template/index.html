Medical billing RFP template and vendor evaluation scorecard

By the Medical Billing Team editors · Published November 7, 2026 · Updated November 7, 2026 · How we research and review

This site is operated by Alliance Global Solutions, a provider of remote medical billing and revenue cycle staff. We may contact you about our services if you request it.

Short answer: A useful medical billing request for proposal makes vendors price the same work and show evidence for their answers. Start with a one-page scope, ask the 20 questions below, resolve mandatory requirements, then compare the remaining proposals with the 100-point scorecard. Copy these tables into your own document; no signup is required.

This is an original procurement worksheet from Medical Billing Team, operated by Alliance Global Solutions. The weights are suggested editorial choices, not industry benchmarks. Use the same process when evaluating AGS. The worksheet supports purchasing discussions; your legal and compliance advisers should review the actual agreements.

1. Copy this scope brief

Fill in business-level information. Approximate or aggregate volumes are enough for an initial conversation. Do not put patient names, insurance IDs, claim images or other patient information in a general RFP or website form.

FieldYour requirement
Organization and contactBusiness name, contact name and work email
Service modelDedicated staff, managed billing service, or a defined project
GoalThe specific queue, coverage gap or operating problem to address
Work includedFor example: unpaid insurance claim follow-up and next-action notes
Work excludedFor example: coding changes, refunds and write-offs without approval
Specialty and payer mixSpecialty and aggregate payer categories; no patient-level details
WorkloadIncoming items per week or month, existing backlog and age bands
SystemsPractice management system, clearinghouse and required portals
CoverageTime zone, hours, languages and holiday requirements
OwnershipYour supervisor, vendor contact and exception approver
ReportingMeasures, definitions, reporting frequency and access to underlying reports
TimelineProposal deadline, decision date and proposed onboarding period
Commercial assumptionsHours or workload priced, minimum term and requested fee breakdown

If the service model is undecided, start with the outsourcing models comparison and staffing agency guide. Ask vendors to price alternatives separately so the scope remains visible.

2. Ask these 20 questions

Request a written answer and the named evidence for each question. Record exceptions rather than treating a sales presentation as a completed response.

Scope, people and supervision

  1. Which tasks are included and excluded? Attach a responsibility matrix naming the vendor and client owners.
  2. Who will perform the work, and from where? Identify the contracting entity, staff locations and any subcontractors.
  3. How will you demonstrate relevant experience? Describe work on our specialty, payer types and systems; provide a fictional work sample or assessment plan.
  4. Who manages daily priorities and quality? Name the supervisory role and provide a sample review schedule.
  5. What happens during leave, turnover or volume spikes? Explain coverage, replacement terms, training responsibility and any extra charges.

Access, safeguards and contract requirements

  1. What agreements apply before anyone accesses protected information? Supply the proposed BAA and identify subcontractor arrangements for review.
  2. How are accounts created, restricted and removed? Walk through approval, individual credentials, access review and offboarding.
  3. How is remote work secured? Explain device management, authentication, downloads, printing, physical workspace and logging.
  4. What happens after a security incident? Identify contacts, escalation steps and the proposed contractual reporting timeline.
  5. Which software or AI tools would touch our information? List tools, their purpose, who operates them and the approval process for changes.

HHS's business associate contract guidance explains agreement provisions concerning permitted uses, safeguards, reporting, subcontractors and return or destruction of information when feasible. A questionnaire or high score does not establish compliance. Review proposed contracts and controls with your adviser. For more discussion, use our outsourced billing HIPAA guide.

Workflow and measurable performance

  1. How do you prioritize work nearing a deadline? Show how payer-specific filing and appeal dates are recorded, checked and escalated.
  2. How do you distinguish rejections, denials and unpaid claims? Provide a sample status report and the definitions used.
  3. What counts as a completed task? Explain whether an item was reviewed, corrected, submitted, followed up or paid.
  4. How is quality measured? Describe sample selection, error categories, reviewer responsibility and correction tracking.
  5. Can we reconcile your reports to our systems? Show a fictional example with a reporting period, source and denominator for each percentage.

Deadlines need their own workflow. For example, Medicare generally requires filing with the correct Medicare Administrative Contractor within one calendar year of service; exceptions and other payer rules require separate review. A vendor should demonstrate how it keeps the applicable rules current. CMS timely filing guidance.

Price, onboarding and exit

  1. What is the exact fee basis? State per-person, hourly, per-claim, percentage-of-collections or other terms, and define the billable unit.
  2. What costs are additional? Itemize software, implementation, equipment, overtime, coverage, management and any minimum charges.
  3. What does onboarding require from us? Supply a proposed schedule with access, training and acceptance checkpoints.
  4. How does the engagement end? Describe notice, fees, data export, open-work handoff, access removal and information handling.
  5. What can we independently verify? Offer relevant references with permission and identify which claims are supported by documents, demonstrations or contractual commitments.

3. Resolve mandatory requirements before scoring

Set these requirements before opening proposals. The exact requirements depend on your organization; these are examples to adapt with the people responsible for compliance and operations.

RequirementDecision to record
Necessary agreements and permitted staff locationsAccepted, needs clarification, or cannot proceed
Approved access and security arrangementAccepted, needs clarification, or cannot proceed
Required schedule and essential scopeAccepted, needs clarification, or cannot proceed
Reporting access and workable exit termsAccepted, needs clarification, or cannot proceed

Resolve a material gap before award. A lower price or stronger interview should not offset a requirement your organization has made mandatory.

4. Use this 100-point vendor scorecard

Method: Rate each category from 0 to 5, then calculate weighted points = category weight × rating ÷ 5. Add the weighted points for a total out of 100. Choose your weights before scoring; if you change them, rescore every vendor consistently. Retain the evidence, reviewer and date beside each rating.

CategorySuggested weightEvidence to compare
Role, specialty and payer fit20Relevant work examples, candidate assessment and scope exceptions
Safeguards and access process20Reviewed documentation and a demonstrated access lifecycle
Workflow and quality control20Queue rules, escalation examples and a quality review method
Reporting and visibility15Reconciled sample report with definitions and source systems
Total cost and commercial clarity15Itemized like-for-like quote and understandable contract terms
Onboarding, continuity and exit10Named owners, training plan, coverage commitments and handoff process
Total100Apply the same evidence standard to every proposal

Rating anchors: 0 = no usable evidence; 1 = assertion only; 2 = partial documentation with material gaps; 3 = documented approach meeting the stated need; 4 = approach supported by a relevant demonstration or corroboration; 5 = verified fit with clear commitments and no material unresolved gap in that category. These are procurement judgments, not certifications.

Worked hypothetical example: A vendor earns 4 out of 5 for role fit, weighted at 20. Its points for that category are 20 × 4 ÷ 5 = 16. Repeat for all six categories. This example does not rate AGS or any other company.

For the cost category, normalize the quotes first with our staffing cost worksheet and state cost calculator. Compare staff capacity and management obligations as well as price.

5. Agree the pilot reporting worksheet

Select measures that match the assigned work. This worksheet proposes definitions to agree with the vendor; it does not prescribe universal targets or promise collections results.

MeasureDefinition to agreeWhy it matters
Starting and ending queueSame queue, period and included statuses; reconcile arrivals and exitsSeparates backlog movement from new work
Completed actionsCount by action type, with rules for repeat touchesDistinguishes meaningful work from repeated activity
Quality reviewReviewed actions, actions with errors and severity; record sample methodMakes the numerator and denominator inspectable
Blocked itemsReason, owner, next action and age of blockReveals where the practice or payer must act
Deadline exposureItems approaching the applicable verified deadlineMakes urgent work visible
Collections, if relevantPayments attributed to the agreed cohort and period, with exclusions statedLimits misleading before-and-after comparisons

A small or selectively chosen quality sample can mislead. Record how the sample was chosen and avoid presenting its result as the error rate of the entire operation. Compare periods using consistent definitions, then discuss payer delays, documentation gaps and other factors outside the assigned team's control.

FAQ

What should a medical billing RFP include?

Include the service model, work scope, approximate volumes, systems, payer mix, coverage hours, responsibilities, security requirements, reporting definitions, pricing assumptions and exit terms. Ask vendors to answer the same questions and attach supporting evidence.

Can I use this RFP for remote billing staff?

Yes. For staffing, specify the roles and hours you need, who directs the work, which tools you provide and how candidates will be assessed. For a managed billing service, define the functions and outcomes the vendor is responsible for delivering.

How do I score medical billing vendors?

Resolve mandatory requirements first. Then rate each weighted category from zero to five based on documented evidence and multiply its weight by the rating divided by five. The suggested weights on this page total 100; they are an editorial starting point, not an industry standard or vendor ranking.

Should I send patient records with an RFP?

Use aggregate business volumes and fictional work samples for initial proposals. Do not attach patient records or claim-level data to a general inquiry. Any later sharing of protected information needs an approved purpose, appropriate agreements and a secure channel.

Is the lowest billing quote the best value?

Compare quotes only after matching scope, hours, fee basis and included costs. Add software, setup, management and exit costs. A low quote with an undefined queue, missing coverage or unclear reporting may not meet the same requirement as another proposal.
