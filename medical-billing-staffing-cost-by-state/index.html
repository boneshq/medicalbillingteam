Short answer: A full-time in-house medical biller costs about $70,600 a year in wages and employer-paid benefits at the US median, and between about $56,500 (Arkansas) and $93,700 (District of Columbia) depending on the state. That is the May 2025 BLS median hourly wage for billing and posting clerks ($23.32 nationally), multiplied by the BLS benefits load for office and administrative support staff (1.4564, June 2026) and 2,080 hours. The tables below give that figure for every state and four billing-office roles, the calculator runs it for your headcount, and the last section puts labelled remote and offshore pay and vendor prices next to it.

This page exists because "what does a biller cost" has a different answer in Jackson, Mississippi than in San Jose, California, and because most cost comparisons online quote one national number against a vendor's price with no sources. Every figure here carries its publisher and date. For the three staffing models themselves, start with our medical billing staffing cost guide; this page is the data behind it.

What the numbers are, and what they are not

  • Wages: median hourly wage by state and occupation from the Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS), May 2025, released May 15, 2026. The median covers every employer in the state that employs the occupation (physician offices, hospitals, billing companies, insurers), and every experience level. Employment counts come from the same release.
  • Benefits load: the BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation (ECEC), June 2026, released September 9, 2026. For private-industry office and administrative support occupations, total compensation was $36.73 an hour, of which $25.22 was wages and salaries and $11.51 (31.3%) was benefits: paid leave, insurance, retirement and legally required costs such as Social Security and unemployment insurance. We use the ratio 36.73 ÷ 25.22 = 1.4564 as the load factor.
  • Hours: 2,080 a year, the BLS convention for a year-round, full-time job.
  • Not included: recruiting, training time, practice management and clearinghouse seats, equipment, workspace and the manager's time. Our staffing cost worksheet adds those. The loaded figure here is a floor, not the whole cost.
  • Occupations: BLS has no "medical biller" line. We use 43-3021 Billing and Posting Clerks for billers, 29-2072 Medical Records Specialists (the category that includes medical coders) for coders, 43-6013 Medical Secretaries and Administrative Assistants for front-office and billing-office support, and 43-4051 Customer Service Representatives for patient-account and call-handling roles.
  • Gaps: Nevada is missing from this first release because the BLS API pull that built the dataset was cut off by the daily request limit; it will be added on the next refresh. The 10th, 25th, 75th and 90th percentile wages, which would serve as an experience proxy, are missing for the same reason.

Fully loaded cost of a medical biller by state

Highest wages for billers (BLS 43-3021, May 2025) are in the District of Columbia ($30.93 an hour, about $93,700 a year loaded), California ($27.05, $81,900), Massachusetts ($26.98, $81,700), Washington ($26.96, $81,700) and Minnesota ($26.83, $81,300). Lowest are Arkansas ($18.65, $56,500), West Virginia ($18.89, $57,200), Alabama ($19.00, $57,600), Mississippi ($19.80, $60,000) and Louisiana ($20.76, $62,900). The most billers work in California (41,290), New York (32,770), Texas (31,150), Florida (28,560) and Illinois (21,010).

Loaded annual cost for four billing-office roles by state

Nationally in May 2025 the median hourly wage was $23.32 for billers (loaded about $70,600 a year), $24.59 for medical records specialists including coders ($74,500), $22.08 for medical secretaries ($66,900) and $21.53 for customer service representatives ($65,200). Medical records specialists (the coder category) out-earn billers in 44 of the 50 states and districts on this page; the gap in median hourly wage is widest in Wisconsin ($5.79), Hawaii ($5.74), New Mexico ($5.46) and Rhode Island ($5.31).

Remote and offshore pay and prices at the same experience level

Two kinds of numbers get mixed up in every "offshore is 70% cheaper" claim: what the worker is paid, and what a client pays a vendor for a dedicated person. They are not the same thing, and neither is directly comparable to the loaded in-house cost above without adjustment. We keep them in separate tables. Currency conversions use the open ExchangeRate-API rates on October 30, 2026 (1 USD = 63.81 PHP, 95.29 INR, 3,164 COP), cross-checked against the Federal Reserve H.10 release of October 26, 2026 for INR (94.94); the H.10 does not carry PHP or COP. Philippine pay normally includes a statutory 13th-month payment, which none of these sources states it includes.

Experience: what can and cannot be matched yet

The India Payscale data is the only series here with experience bands: a billing specialist with 1 to 4 years earns about $2,785 a year, one with 5 to 9 years about $4,198, a 51% step. The BLS state medians have no experience split; the OEWS 25th and 75th percentile wages are the usual proxy, and we will add them when the state file is loaded. Until then, remember that the state median mixes every experience level, and ask a vendor which experience level its quoted price covers.

How to read the gap honestly

  1. Compare price to loaded cost, not pay to pay. A client pays the vendor's all-in price, not the worker's salary. At the US median, in-house wages and benefits are about $5,880 a month ($70,600 ÷ 12); Connext's published ranges above run $900 to $2,000 a month for a dedicated full-time biller in India or the Philippines; the other vendor rows are quoted hourly or yearly. That is the gap before your add-ons.
  2. Add your own costs to the remote side. Software seats, a virtual desktop or VPN with multi-factor authentication (what to require), and the manager's time to assign and review work. The calculator has a field for this.
  3. Add the hidden costs to the in-house side. Recruiting, a vacancy month while A/R ages, turnover and the owner's time. Those are in the staffing cost guide.
  4. Check the scope. A vendor's "biller" may post payments only, or may work denials and appeals; our A/R specialist and denial management guides describe the work so you can match it.
  5. Decide what control is worth. Dedicated remote staff work in your systems under your direction; a billing company runs the process for a percentage of collections. The three outsourcing models compares them, and offshore medical billers covers the HIPAA and payer-contract questions that apply once staff are outside the US.

FAQ

How much does an in-house medical biller cost per year?

Using the BLS national median of $23.32 an hour for billing and posting clerks (May 2025) and the BLS benefits load for office and administrative support staff (June 2026), a full-time in-house biller costs about $70,600 a year in wages and employer-paid benefits. The state tables on this page range from about $56,500 in Arkansas to $93,700 in the District of Columbia. Recruiting, software, equipment, workspace and supervision come on top.

Which states have the highest and lowest medical billing wages?

In the May 2025 BLS data, the highest median hourly wages for billing and posting clerks are in the District of Columbia ($30.93), California ($27.05), Massachusetts ($26.98), Washington ($26.96) and Minnesota ($26.83). The lowest are Arkansas ($18.65), West Virginia ($18.89), Alabama ($19.00), Mississippi ($19.80) and Louisiana ($20.76).

What does fully loaded cost mean on this page?

Wages plus the employer's cost of benefits, using the BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation ratio for private-industry office and administrative support workers: $36.73 of total compensation per hour for every $25.22 of wages in June 2026, a load factor of 1.4564. It does not include recruiting, training, software, equipment, workspace or management time, so the real cost of an in-house hire is higher.

How much cheaper are offshore medical billers?

It depends on what you compare. Worker pay in the Philippines and India runs a few hundred dollars a month in the self-reported data on this page, but a client does not pay worker pay. Vendor-published prices for a dedicated full-time biller in India or the Philippines run from about $900 to $2,000 a month in Connext's published ranges; a vendor's price includes its overhead and margin, so it is the number to compare with the loaded in-house cost. Compare a vendor's all-in monthly price, plus your own software, access and management costs, with the loaded in-house cost for your state.

Can I compare staff with the same experience level?

Only partly for now. The BLS state medians cover workers of all experience levels, and the 25th and 75th percentile wages that would act as an experience proxy are not yet on this page. The India figures from Payscale are split into early-career (1 to 4 years) and mid-career (5 to 9 years) bands, which gives one like-for-like view. We will add the US percentiles when the BLS state file is loaded.
