Who this site helps

  • Solo and small practices where the office manager is also the biller, the scheduler and the person who calls payers.
  • Specialty groups such as behavioral health, physical therapy, chiropractic, labs and ambulatory surgery centers (ASCs), where payer rules and authorizations are specialty-specific.
  • Multi-location groups that need consistent billing processes across sites.
  • Practice and billing managers who need more hands on the A/R, denial or authorization queues without adding local headcount.
  • Medical billing companies that need to add trained staff as they win new clients.

Common billing problems we write about

  • Denials that never get reworked. In a vendor survey of billing professionals, 40% reported an increase in denials over the prior year (Tebra, 2025). Federal transparency data show HealthCare.gov insurers denied 20% of in-network claims in 2023 (KFF).
  • Aging A/R. Claims nobody has followed up on after 30, 60 or 90 days.
  • Prior authorization workload. Physicians surveyed by the AMA reported an average of 39 prior authorization requests per physician per week, taking about 13 hours of physician and staff time (AMA).
  • Eligibility surprises. Coverage changes discovered after the visit, not before.
  • Hiring and turnover. In the same vendor survey, 38% of billers said they struggle to find qualified candidates (Tebra, 2025).

The remote billing team model, explained

A remote billing team is not a software product and not a full billing takeover. It is dedicated people working your queues:

  • They work in your systems. Your practice management system, clearinghouse and payer portals, with access you grant and can revoke.
  • They follow your rules. Your fee schedules, your write-off policy, your escalation path.
  • They are dedicated. The same people every day, so they learn your payers and your providers.
  • You keep control. You own the process and the payer relationships. The staffing provider recruits, employs, trains and supports the staff.

Typical roles include billing specialists, A/R follow-up specialists, eligibility and benefits verification (VOB) staff, prior authorization coordinators, payment posters, denial follow-up specialists and admissions or intake support.

HIPAA comes first

If anyone outside your practice will touch patient data, HIPAA is the first question, not the last. Before remote or offshore billing staff log in to anything, you should expect:

  • A business associate agreement (BAA) with the agency, because a company that handles billing involving protected health information on your behalf is a business associate under HIPAA (HHS).
  • Minimum necessary, role-based access. Staff see only what their job requires.
  • Strong access controls: unique logins, multi-factor authentication, secure remote access (VPN or virtual desktop) and no local downloads or printing.
  • Documented training, risk analysis and a breach-reporting process.

One more thing to know: there is no official "HIPAA certification" for companies or staff. HHS's Office for Civil Rights states that it does not endorse, certify or recommend specific technology or products (HHS). Be cautious of anyone who claims to be "HIPAA certified."

Short FAQ

What is a remote medical billing team? Dedicated billing and RCM staff who work remotely in your systems and under your workflows. You direct the work; the staffing provider handles recruiting, employment and support.

Is this the same as hiring a billing company? No. A billing company usually takes over the whole function. A staffing model gives you dedicated people and you keep control.

What does the free assessment involve? A short conversation about your workload and gaps. No obligation, and no patient information needed.

Can remote staff work within HIPAA? Yes, when the right agreements and safeguards are in place. There is no official HIPAA certification, so ask about the specific safeguards instead. See the HIPAA guide.
